# 0044 Screenshots and video recording

Status: accepted (implements ticket #44)

**What**
- F12 (or the Capture page of the overlay) saves the presented frame as PNG to `<data>/screenshots/screenshot_NNNN.png`; F10 (or the page) starts and stops a video, saved as H.264/AAC (or MPEG-4/AAC, see below) `<data>/videos/video_NNNN.mp4`. Pictures are at the internal resolution (ADR 0019/0020 resolution option), as the frame dumps are. `capture.overlay` in `settings.toml` (checkbox on the page) draws the overlay (menu, statistics, "REC" indicator and messages) into them; the default is the bare game picture. Messages (file saved, errors) and a REC timer are drawn in the bottom-left corner of the window.
- Headless: `FEPOR_CAPTURE=shot@N,record@A-B` presses the screenshot key at game frame N and records from frame A to B (used for checks; `FEPOR_OVERLAY` and `capture.overlay` give a screenshot with the overlay).

**How**
- `fepor_core::capture::Capture` (owned by `overlay::Shared`) is the meeting point of the window thread (hotkeys, buttons), the renderer (`Renderer::capture_frame`, next to the `FEPOR_DUMP_FRAMES` code, which shares the readback and the RGB conversion), the audio path (`fepor_audio::CaptureSink`, a tap beside the SDL and WAV sinks that gets the audio at full volume and is always present, also with `FEPOR_NO_AUDIO`) and a writer thread. Only real game frames are recorded, not interpolated images. With the overlay option the overlay is painted on a copy of the EFB, so the game's picture and the window's input are untouched.
- Encoder decision: an **external `ffmpeg`** (`FEPOR_FFMPEG`, else next to the executable, else the `PATH`), not a bundled pure-Rust encoder. A pure-Rust H.264 encoder does not exist at a usable quality, MPEG-4/MJPEG ones are large and slow, and every candidate adds a heavy dependency and release-archive size; ffmpeg is ubiquitous and a player who records can install it. Without it, the recording refuses to start with a message saying so (screenshots need nothing). The encoder is picked from `ffmpeg -encoders`: libx264, else libopenh264, else MPEG-4 part 2 (some distributions ship ffmpeg without x264).
- Pipeline: raw RGB frames through a pipe to ffmpeg into a Matroska part file, the audio into a WAV part file, and at stop ffmpeg muxes them (video copied, audio AAC) into the `.mp4` on its own thread; the parts are deleted. Quitting (window close, Quit page, the game ending) waits for the mux.
- Synchronisation: both streams are timed by the wall clock from the start of the recording. The game presents 30 or 60 frames a second, so each picture is repeated until the next arrives, giving a constant 60000/1001 fps; the audio is paced by the same clock (AI DMA timer) and a hole of more than 100 ms (late guest) is filled with silence. A change of picture size is fitted (nearest neighbour) to the first one (rounded down to even); a change of audio rate is resampled to the first one. 

**Limits**
- Recording while the frame rate is sped up or the guest runs slower than real time follows the wall clock, so the video then plays the game at real speed with repeated frames. Audio lags the picture by up to the pacing jitter (tens of ms); not measured against a reference.
- Checked: headless 5 s recording (ffprobe: h264 608x448 60000/1001 fps 5.255 s, aac 32 kHz stereo 5.18 s) and screenshots with and without overlay. The test machine's ffmpeg has libopenh264 but no libx264, so the OpenH264 path was exercised. Not checked: windowed hotkeys on a real display, the libx264 and MPEG-4 fallback paths, Windows/macOS.
